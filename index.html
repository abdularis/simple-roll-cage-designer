    Rod and bend radius

    E and yield are only used by the strength check. Left empty they use typical values for the material (shown greyed): check your supplier's figures. Brazing heat softens the rod near the joints. CLR is measured to the rod centre: former radius + half the OD. A 17 mm diameter former with 3 mm rod gives 8.5 + 1.5 = 10 mm.

    Print pack

    Full-size pages to work from with a hand bender and a ruler: a parts list, a test bend that measures your bender, one page per bent part (marking strip, bend steps, and the finished shape to check against, with every straight length), the straight pieces, and full-size side, top and front views to hold parts while you join them. Print at 100 % ("actual size") in landscape and check the 50 mm bar.

    After the test bend on page 2, enter the springback and mark offset under Rod & bender in the tool bar, then print again so every page matches your bender.

    Strength check

    The % is how close a spot comes to bending for good in that crash. Below 100% it springs back; at 100% it stays bent. 50% means the hit could be about twice as hard before that happens.

      Weak spots

        Braces that help

          Loads and mounts (starting guesses: set them for your car)

          How it works: the cage is treated as rods brazed rigidly together and fixed at the mounts, and each crash is a steady push of car weight × the factor, shared by the tubes that touch the ground first. Real crashes are sudden and real braze joints can be weaker than the rod, so read the figures as a ranking of where the cage is weakest, not a promise it will survive. Mounts start on the tube corners that sit on a keep-out marked as a mount surface (the chassis plate); use the Mounts tool to change them.

          Keep-out zones

          Positions and footprints are measured from the manual's top views (p.30 chassis, p.31 assembled car), scaled so the wheelbase is LC's 230 mm. The drawings are orthographic, so lengths and widths are good to about ±2 mm. Heights are estimates: the manual has no side view. Measure the tops of the shock towers, top deck, motor, spur cover and wing on your car and correct the Z and Height columns. Make the front wheels larger if you want room for steering lock. Untick "Check" for surfaces the cage is meant to touch, like the chassis plate.

          From screen to brazed cage

          1. Measure the heights. Correct the heights in the Car keep-outs tab so the clearance check means something.
          2. Set up the bender. Under Rod & bender in the tool bar, enter your rod and the bender's bend radius (CLR).
          3. Draw tubes with the mouse. Use Draw or + Add, then drag tubes and handles into place. Keep each tube flat (all bends in one plane) where you can: flat tubes get one full-size shape to bend against. A 3D tube prints one flat drawing per section plus a protractor for the twists, but it is harder to bend accurately.
          4. Clear the reds. Fix any segment too short for the radius and any tube that hits the car.
          5. Check the strength (optional). Press Strength in the tool bar. Tubes turn green → amber → red by how hard they work in a crash, and the Strength tab lists the weak spots and the braces that help most. It is a ranking, not a guarantee: the loads and rod figures are starting guesses.
          6. Print the pack at 100 % and do its test bend once: it gives you the springback and mark offset for your bender. Enter them and print again.
          7. Cut, mark, bend, check. Each part's page walks through it: copy the B lines from the strip, put each line on the bender's 0, pull to the angle shown, lay the tube on the full-size drawing after every bend, then trim and measure across the ends.
          8. Braze on the template. Pin the pieces over the printout on a ceramic or steel plate, never bare paper or wood at the joint.

          Line on 0 (in Bend numbers) is where to draw the line that goes on the bender's 0, measured from the start end (point 1) of the cut rod, extra length included. It is the start of the bend, moved by the mark offset. Centre mark is the middle of the bend. Bend to is the angle to bend before release so springback lands on the target.

          Rotate first is how far to turn the tube in the bender before that bend, looking from the start end along the tube (↻ clockwise, ↺ anticlockwise). 0° and 180° mean the tube stays flat. For a 3D tube the print pack adds a full-size protractor for these turns and one flat drawing per section to check against. A mirrored piece uses the same marks with every turn the other way.
